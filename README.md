# LncRNAs-epigenetics-Drugs-schistosoma-mansoni
Inhibition of chromatin modifying enzymes EZH2 and LSD1 in Schistosoma mansoni decreases parasite fertility and affects the expression of a set of lncRNAs

Schistosomiasis remains a major public health challenge, with praziquantel as the sole therapeutic option despite its limited activity against juvenile worms and the looming threat of resistance. Epigenetic regulation and long non-coding RNAs (lncRNAs) have emerged as key components of Schistosoma mansoni biology, yet their interplay remains poorly understood. Here, we investigated the effect of suppression of the chromatin-modifying enzymes EZH2 or LSD1 on the phenotypic characteristics of adult S. mansoni using two complementary approaches: (i) pharmacological inhibition of the activity of either enzyme; or, in parallel, (ii) RNAi-mediated knockdown of the mRNA coding for either enzyme. Treatment with the EZH2 inhibitor GSK343 or the LSD1 inhibitor MC3935 disrupted parasite viability, motility, pairing, and fertility more profoundly than the RNAi-mediated knockdown of the genes coding for the enzymes, indicating broader effects of small-molecule inhibition. RNA-Seq re-analysis revealed that both drugs trigger extensive, sex-biased transcriptional remodeling, including the differential expression of drug-responsive lncRNAs. Single-cell and developmental stage expression profiling of four drug-responsive long intergenic non-coding RNAs (lincRNAs) uncovered distinct spatial and temporal enrichment patterns, while RT-qPCR validation confirmed that several lincRNAs, especially SmLINC101519-IBu and SmLINC133371-IBu, are responsive to both EZH2 and LSD1 perturbations. Together, our findings identify a set of lincRNA candidates for further functional characterization as associated with chromatin regulation in S. mansoni.
